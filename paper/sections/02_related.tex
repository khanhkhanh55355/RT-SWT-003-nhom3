\section{Related Work}

\subsection{LLM-Based Test and Scenario Generation}

Recent progress in large language models (LLMs) has sparked growing interest in using them to automate software testing tasks, including generating test cases and behavior-driven development (BDD) scenarios. Prior work has evaluated models such as GPT-3, GPT-4, and CodeBERT for their ability to translate natural-language artifacts into executable testing assets \cite{wang2024llm, yuan2023chattester, siddiq2023exploring}. For example, Wang et al. showed that ChatGPT can produce useful unit tests, but also reported that the generated code often requires human intervention to compile and run correctly \cite{wang2024llm}. In a different line of work, CodeBERT-based approaches have been used to align requirements text with code, though they typically struggle with the strict structural constraints imposed by domain-specific languages such as Gherkin \cite{bareiss2022code}.

Existing evaluations commonly rely on BLEU, exact string matching, compilation/pass rates, and human judgment. While these measures are convenient, they often miss the semantic intent of BDD scenarios. As a result, many studies still depend heavily on qualitative expert scoring because automated metrics tailored to scenario generation remain limited. In a closely related study, Rathnayake et al. assessed GPT-4 for zero-shot Gherkin generation and reported an average expert score of 4.63/5.0 \cite{rathnayake2026llm}. These results are encouraging, but the reliance on manual scoring makes large-scale, reproducible evaluation difficult.

\subsection{BDD and Gherkin in Software Testing}

Behavior-Driven Development (BDD) is widely adopted in agile development because it helps technical and non-technical stakeholders align on expected system behavior through a shared, domain-level language, most commonly expressed in Gherkin \cite{smart2014bdd}. Prior studies suggest that BDD can reduce miscommunication and improve alignment between implemented behavior and business requirements \cite{solis2011study}. However, writing and maintaining Gherkin scenarios remains labor-intensive. Some reports estimate that it can account for 30\%--50\% of overall testing effort, which becomes a major bottleneck in fast-moving teams \cite{irshad2021systematic}.

To reduce this burden, researchers have explored automation. Earlier approaches relied on rule-based NLP pipelines and template-driven generation, parsing user stories into fixed Gherkin structures using part-of-speech tags and syntactic patterns \cite{wang2021automated, alfadel2021empirical}. Although effective for tightly controlled inputs, these methods tend to be brittle and do not generalize well to the diverse and messy user stories found in industrial practice. More importantly, across both rule-based and early AI-driven methods, large-scale validation has typically lacked robust, automated quantitative metrics, leaving a gap in objective assessment of scenario-generation quality.

\subsection*{}

In contrast to prior work that primarily uses human scoring or BLEU-style similarity, this paper evaluates GPT-4o zero-shot Gherkin generation using cosine semantic similarity with a fixed threshold ($\geq 0.85$) and executable-syntax rate, and validates the findings with Wilcoxon and Binomial significance tests on an industrial dataset.
