\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Behavior-Driven Development (BDD) encourages teams to express expected system behavior in structured, human-readable scenarios, typically written in Gherkin and linked to agile user stories~\cite{cucumber2023gherkin}.
In practice, authoring these scenarios remains labor-intensive: practitioners must translate informal requirements into syntactically valid Given--When--Then steps while preserving domain terminology and testable intent.
Rathnayake et al.~\cite{rathnayake2026llm} recently released an industrial dataset of 500 user-story and manually authored Gherkin pairs from IntelligenceBank products, highlighting both the value of high-quality reference scenarios and the effort required to produce them at scale.

Large language models (LLMs) have shown promise for automating natural-language artifacts in software engineering, including requirements refinement and test-related text generation.
Rathnayake et al.~\cite{rathnayake2026llm} evaluated GPT-4 in a zero-shot setting on their dataset and reported a mean expert rating of 4.63 out of 5.0 for generated scenarios, suggesting that modern LLMs can produce plausible Gherkin from user stories without task-specific training.

However, such human ratings alone do not establish whether model outputs meet predefined, reproducible quality gates that teams could embed in a continuous evaluation pipeline.

Prior work on LLM-based BDD generation rarely combines \emph{automated} quality metrics, \emph{statistical hypothesis testing}, and \emph{fixed acceptance thresholds} in a single empirical design.
In our review of 13 closely related studies, seven lacked one or more of these elements, making cross-study comparison and objective pass/fail decisions difficult.
This gap limits the extent to which practitioners can trust zero-shot generation as a drop-in substitute for manual scenario authoring under explicit quality contracts.

This paper addresses that gap through a controlled replication-style evaluation of GPT-4o on a curated subset of the Rathnayake et al. dataset.
Our contributions are threefold:
\begin{itemize}
  \item We present, to our knowledge, the first empirical evaluation of \textbf{GPT-4o zero-shot Gherkin generation} from user stories using automated metrics---cosine semantic similarity ($\geq 0.85$) with OpenAI \texttt{text-embedding-3-large} embeddings~\cite{openai2024embeddings} and executable syntax rate ($\geq 80\%$)---together with one-tailed statistical tests and pre-registered thresholds.
  \item We report experimental results on $N{=}100$ pairs spanning four IntelligenceBank domains: \textbf{RQ1 (semantic similarity) was not achieved}, whereas \textbf{RQ2 (executable syntax) was achieved}.
  \item We release a \textbf{public replication package}---including sampled data, generation scripts, API logs, analysis notebooks, and statistical summaries---at \url{https://github.com/khanhkhanh55355/RT-SWT-003-nhom3}.
\end{itemize}
